There’s a point in every project where the big decisions are made and the small ones begin to matter. The rhythm of a sentence. The way a page responds to a narrow screen. The quiet confidence of leaving something out.

I used to think details were decoration—something to add at the end, when time allowed. Now I think of them as a way of showing care. Not every detail needs to be noticed. It just needs to make the experience feel a little more natural.

## Start by paying attention

Before polishing anything, I try to use the thing as a real person would. Where do I hesitate? What feels louder than it needs to be? Which parts make me smile?

Those observations are usually more useful than another round of abstract opinions. They bring the work back to the people it is meant to serve.

## Leave room for good things

Making space is a design decision. It gives content room to breathe, gives a reader somewhere to rest, and gives a team a clearer sense of what matters.

The details I remember most are rarely flashy. They’re the moments when something simply feels right.
