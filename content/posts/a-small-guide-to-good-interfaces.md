Good interfaces don’t need to be complicated to feel considered. Most of the time, the fundamentals do the heavy lifting.

## Make the next step clear

People shouldn’t have to guess what will happen when they click a button. Use familiar patterns, plain language, and a clear visual hierarchy.

## Give feedback

A useful interface acknowledges what just happened. A saved state, a helpful error message, or a button that responds to input all help people feel oriented.

## Design for real conditions

Text gets longer. Screens get smaller. Networks get slower. Build layouts and interactions that stay useful when things aren’t perfect.

## Check the details

Keyboard access, readable contrast, meaningful labels, and generous touch targets are part of good design—not a final checklist.

The best interface is often the one that lets you focus on what you came to do.
